1. Classical / Foundational texts (chart calculation + interpretation)

Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra (BPHS) — Sage Parashara; foundational text (~classical era, compiled later). Covers planets, houses, rashis, divisional charts (vargas), Vimshottari and other dashas, yogas, remedies. The backbone of Parashari astrology. Access: Public-domain Sanskrit available; the widely used R. Santhanam English translation (Ranjan Publications) is copyrighted — purchase only. Sanskrit originals on archive.org / Wikisource.
Brihat Jataka — Varahamihira (~6th century CE). Concise natal astrology, yogas, planetary effects. Access: Older translations (e.g., N. Chidambaram Iyer) are largely public domain on archive.org.
Saravali — Kalyana Varma (~10th century). Detailed planetary combinations and yogas. Access: Santhanam translation copyrighted; older editions may be on archive.org.
Phaladeepika — Mantreswara. Practical predictive rules, yogas, dashas. Access: V. Subrahmanya Sastri translation often public domain (archive.org).
Jaimini Sutras — Maharishi Jaimini. Alternate (Jaimini) system: chara karakas, padas, Chara dasha. Access: Older translations on archive.org; modern editions copyrighted.
Hora Sara, Sarvartha Chintamani, Jataka Parijata — additional classical predictive references; mixed copyright, older translations often public domain.

2. Computational / technical references (the math)

Lahiri's Indian Ephemeris / Rashtriya Panchang — standard ayanamsa (Lahiri/Chitrapaksha) and ephemeris data. Access: Copyrighted, purchase.
Swiss Ephemeris (Astrodienst) — high-precision planetary positions; widely used in astrology software. Access: Open source (AGPL) / commercial license — astro.com/swisseph. This is your most practical computational backbone.
Surya Siddhanta — classical Hindu astronomical text (planetary motion, time cycles). Access: Burgess translation, public domain (archive.org).
Reference topics to cover in corpus: ayanamsa systems, house systems (whole-sign vs. Sripati/Placidus), Vimshottari/Ashtottari/Chara dasha math, varga (D-1 through D-60) derivation, panchanga (tithi, nakshatra, yoga, karana).

3. Modern commentaries & translations (interpretation)

B.V. Raman — Three Hundred Important Combinations, How to Judge a Horoscope, Hindu Predictive Astrology. Influential modern works. Access: Copyrighted, purchase.
K.N. Rao — works on dashas and predictive technique. Copyrighted.
K.S. Krishnamurti — KP system (stellar astrology). Copyrighted.
James Braha, Hart de Fouw & Robert Svoboda (Light on Life) — Western-accessible Vedic texts. Copyrighted.

4. Categorization by use

Chart calculation / reference data: Swiss Ephemeris, Lahiri Ephemeris, Surya Siddhanta, panchanga tables.
Interpretation / predictions: BPHS, Brihat Jataka, Phaladeepika, Saravali, B.V. Raman, K.N. Rao.
Remedies: BPHS (remedial measures sections), modern remedial-astrology texts.
Alternate systems: Jaimini Sutras (Jaimini), Krishnamurti (KP).

Important constraints for your build

Scientific status: Astrology is not scientifically validated. There is no demonstrated causal mechanism, and controlled studies have not shown predictive validity. Your software should present "insights" explicitly as traditional belief / cultural interpretation, not factual prediction, ideally with a visible disclaimer. This matters both ethically and for liability.
Copyright: For your RAG corpus, prefer public-domain Sanskrit originals and pre-1930s translations (often on archive.org) plus the open-source Swiss Ephemeris for computation. Purchase licensed copies of modern works (Raman, Santhanam, Rao) rather than ingesting pirated PDFs — ingesting copyrighted text into a commercial product creates real legal exposure.
Practical architecture split: Keep computation (Swiss Ephemeris, deterministic math) separate from interpretation (RAG over text rules). Charts should be calculated, not generated by an LLM.

Here are 4 additional constraints to add to your build:

Data provenance & versioning: Tag every chunk in your RAG corpus with its source text, translation edition, and translator. Classical rules vary significantly between editions and translations (e.g., a Santhanam vs. Sharma rendering of the same BPHS sloka can differ). Without provenance metadata you can't audit why the software gave a particular interpretation, reconcile contradictory rules, or prove your licensing chain. Store source + edition + page/sloka reference alongside each embedding.

Contradiction & rule-conflict handling: Classical texts frequently disagree (BPHS vs. Jaimini vs. KP use different houses, dashas, and significators). A naive RAG will retrieve conflicting slokas and produce incoherent output. Decide upfront which system(s) the software follows (e.g., Parashari + Vimshottari as default), scope retrieval to that system, and flag when sources conflict rather than blending them silently.

Birth-data accuracy & input validation: Kundali calculations are extremely sensitive to birth time, timezone, and location. A few minutes' error shifts the ascendant and house cusps; wrong historical timezone/DST data corrupts the whole chart. Validate and normalize inputs (geocoding to lat/long, historical timezone database like IANA tz, ayanamsa selection) and surface uncertainty when birth time is approximate ("rectification" needed).

Privacy & data protection: Birth date, time, exact location, and name are personally identifiable and sensitive. If you store client charts you fall under data-protection obligations (India's DPDP Act, GDPR for EU users). Encrypt at rest, minimize retention, get consent, and never send raw client PII into third-party LLM APIs without anonymization and a data-processing agreement.
